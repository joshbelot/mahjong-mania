Status: **baseline walkthrough documented**, with the specific unexercised cases listed below. This is an expert-led simulator audit, not a novice user study.

## Audit-method clarification — 2026-11-01

The planning spec now explicitly permits stepwise XCTest navigation with visual screenshot review (NEXT_WAVES_SPEC §7.1). A missing Simulator.app window alone does not block this method and does not require an Xcode reinstall. The subsequent stepwise walkthrough and remaining limitations are documented below. Describe this as a screenshot-reviewed expert walkthrough, not a manual GUI session or an actual novice study.

The planning agent separately inspected the existing intro and first-question PNGs (`8ABE7DE9-7E0D-4E8A-A7FB-E367C8ADBAB8.png` and `91288BA2-BFA8-4B56-B3E4-1E5F90951492.png` in the screenshot directory below). They confirm the introduction omits copy counts and the visible question asks about 5 Cracks “in a set.” This review validates those two checkpoints only. CoreSimulator's device listing and screenshot capability were also checked; the owner's booted simulator was not modified.

## Stepwise fresh-path observations — 2026-11-01

An isolated fresh iPhone 17 Pro simulator (`F091FA60-D99C-4AA3-9972-8EBF5B73E34D`, iOS 26.5) was launched with no app launch flags. An interactive XCTest relayed one navigation action at a time; the facilitator visually inspected screenshots and chose answers from the displayed questions, without answer metadata or path-unlock shortcuts. Screenshots are in `/private/tmp/terra14-stepwise/`. This is an expert-led walkthrough, and facilitator knowledge was used where teaching was missing.

- Fresh onboarding selected “I'm new” and the Practice Card. The first path showed Today’s Deal, Test out, a daily ring and Start together (`05-path.png`); the first lesson still lacked a 14-tile goal or copy counts (`06-intro.png`, `07-intro-scroll.png`). A wrong answer showed feedback and eventually a retry; the hint icon immediately revealed a hint (`08-q1.png`–`15-return.png`). The tile guide opened and returned to the same question. The retry said “Let's try that one again” but displayed a different dragon/suit question from the original wrong one (`08-q1.png`, `27-reask.png`); investigate whether the underlying retry actually preserves the intended question.
- The first lesson produced level, badge and goal screens before returning to the path (`29-complete.png`–`34-path.png`). Lesson two visibly said “Eight Flowers all count as one tile” (`35-flowers.png`) and asked whether a Joker could be passed before that rule had been taught (`44-q5.png`). The mixed lesson asked the first Charleston direction without teaching it in its introduction (`55-mixed.png`, `64-mixedq5.png`); facilitator knowledge supplied the answer.
- All Unit 1 lessons and the checkpoint were completed in order (`94-complete.png`), opening Unit 2 without an unlock fixture. Unit 2 Sets supplied a visual group-size example (`98-sets.png`), but Colours went from one short paragraph to full card-line comparisons (`118-colours.png`, `119-question.png`). Exposed vs concealed gave a brief X/C distinction (`137-exposed.png`), followed by mixed card questions. All four Unit 2 lessons and its checkpoint were completed naturally (`174-complete.png`, `196-complete.png`). The times on completion screens include the pauses for screenshot inspection and are not novice timing measurements.
- The hand-identification question at `158-question.png` depicted five identical natural 4 Dots and five identical natural 4 Cracks in a quint, despite only four copies of each numbered tile in the set. This is a concrete generated-example defect requiring investigation and a regression test.
- Helper: the Practice Card and Tiles controls were visible on entry (`200-helper.png`). The built-in random deal supplied a 13-tile Charleston rack (`201-rack.png`); the facilitator revealed a pass and closest hands (`203-suggest.png`, `205-hands.png`). The latter shows “7 away” and “8 away” without defining the term there (B11). Switching to Playing, tapping 6 Crack to remove it, then using the keyboard to add it and 1 Crack yielded a 14-tile rack (`207-playing.png`–`210-full.png`); Tiles opened over the rack (`211-tiles.png`).
- In a bounded dead-hand setup using the original Audit card, the rack contained one Flower and its line required a Flower pair. Marking the seven other Flowers as already played (`282-seen.png`, `283-seen-7.png`) made the result show “Not possible” and “DEAD” (`286-scroll.png`), but the result did not explain the missing Flower or counts. A tap on the displayed hand label was unavailable. This reproduces the missing reason aspect of B11; it is not a fresh learner's independently discovered case.
- Game: from the fresh container, created three named players and kept Standard rules (`214-setup.png`, `224-add.png`).
